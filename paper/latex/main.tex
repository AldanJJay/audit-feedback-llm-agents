\abstract{We ask whether audit feedback changes what large language model (LLM) trading agents do or only what they say. Motivated by an earlier exploratory phase (Appendix~\ref{app:preliminary}), this preregistered synthetic-market experiment has a switching fundamental-return driver. It compares five audit conditions. A sign-flip probe reverses a feature's sign and re-asks the same prompt; an identical-prompt null re-ask measures response variability. Precision measures agreement between cited features and features whose reversal changes the action. The strategic-compliance index (SCI) compares citation change with sign-flip-dependence change. Holm adjustment accounts for multiple comparisons. DeepSeek's primary contrasts were evidence-minus-naive precision \texorpdfstring{$-0.014$}{-0.014} (Holm \texorpdfstring{$p=0.368$}{p=0.368}), true-driver sign-flip dependence \texorpdfstring{$-0.062$}{-0.062} (Holm \texorpdfstring{$p=0.014$}{p=0.014}), label SCI \texorpdfstring{$-0.039$}{-0.039} (Holm \texorpdfstring{$p=0.273$}{p=0.273}), and placebo SCI \texorpdfstring{$-0.134$}{-0.134} (Holm \texorpdfstring{$p=4.18\times10^{-5}$}{p=4.18e-5}). Relative to naive, the placebo package increased opposite-driver sign-flip dependence more than citation under the registered SCI. Evidence reduced true-driver sign-flip dependence in Luna (\texorpdfstring{$\Delta=-0.091$}{Delta=-0.091}, Holm \texorpdfstring{$p=0.038$}{p=0.038}). Both contrasts estimate audit packages, not isolated content effects. Instrument checks passed the pooled flip-rate gate only; news-regime discrimination reversed in DeepSeek and was near zero in Haiku. Auditor agreement does not establish process alignment or identify internal reasoning or deceptive intent.}

\section{Introduction}
An auditor may accept an agent's explanation without knowing whether the stated reasons influenced its action. This problem arises when large language models (LLMs) select actions in decision-support and agentic systems. Users may trust an output because of its explanation, and auditors or operators may use that explanation when deciding whether to intervene. In the European Union, Article 13 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, the Artificial Intelligence Act, requires high-risk AI systems to provide sufficient transparency for deployers to interpret their outputs and use them appropriately~\cite{ai_act_2024}. Explanations therefore need evidential value. Our synthetic trading task is not a legal compliance assessment or a claim that every trading application falls within that provision.

An explanation can agree with an evaluator yet say little about the action it accompanies. Generated rationales can omit influential factors~\cite{turpin2023}. Research on self-generated counterfactual explanations and decision explanations raises related concerns~\cite{wang2025,lee2026}. The evaluator may also supply the expected answer. Agreement can then reflect adoption of an authoritative label. We test whether the associated decision changes as well.

Our preregistered confirmatory experiment uses a controlled market. The fundamental component of returns depends on news or momentum and switches repeatedly between them. Agents observe features, trade, and cite their reasons. We measure two kinds of agreement. \emph{Environmental fidelity} means that an agent cites the feature currently driving fundamental returns. \emph{Restricted process alignment} means that cited features agree with features whose sign reversal changes the agent's action. The latter uses a \emph{counterfactual sign-flip probe}: we reverse one feature's sign, keep the rest of the prompt fixed, and re-ask for an action. Environmental fidelity concerns the environment; restricted process alignment concerns the agent's response to this intervention. Neither measure provides direct access to internal reasoning.

An earlier exploratory phase with a different design found that agents cited any auditor-disclosed label with probability 1.000, even when false. The corrective-audit environmental-fidelity gain (0.750 versus 0.627) became inconclusive after we excluded decisions exposed to disclosed labels ($\Delta=0.052$, $p=0.258$). We call this descriptive pattern auditor echo. It motivated us to measure decisions as well as explanations. The preliminary data are never pooled with the confirmatory data and provide no confirmatory evidence. Appendix~\ref{app:preliminary} gives the full design, results, and limitations.

We therefore distinguish citation adoption from decision sensitivity under institutional feedback. Following the exploratory motivation (Appendix~\ref{app:preliminary}), we test whether auditor echo extends to decisions. In DeepSeek, the placebo audit package increased opposite-driver sign-flip dependence more than citation relative to naive under the registered SCI. The evidence audit package also reduced true-driver sign-flip sensitivity relative to naive in the primary model and one replication. Neither comparison isolates feedback content from auditing and fines. We report null results, contrary effects, instrument weaknesses, and model differences alongside these findings.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Explainable AI and explanation faithfulness.}
Research distinguishes models that people can interpret from post hoc explanations, which are generated after a prediction~\cite{rudin2019}. Other work examines what interpretability means~\cite{lipton2018} and surveys explanation methods~\cite{guidotti2018,samek2021,barredo2020}. Social and pragmatic accounts ask what explanations are for and how to evaluate them~\cite{miller2019,paez2019}. Local surrogates such as LIME approximate a prediction near one input~\cite{ribeiro2016}. Additive attribution methods such as SHAP divide a prediction among input features under particular assumptions~\cite{lundberg2017}. Generated rationales require separate evaluation. Chain-of-thought explanations can omit experimentally introduced influences~\cite{turpin2023}, and a model's own counterfactual account need not locate its decision boundary~\cite{wang2025}. Jacovi and Goldberg distinguish faithfulness, or accuracy about the basis for a decision, from plausibility to a reader~\cite{jacovi2020}. We use this distinction to report environmental agreement and behavioral-probe agreement separately.

\paragraph{Counterfactual measurement of process alignment.}
A model can propose a counterfactual explanation without anyone changing its input. Our counterfactual experiment instead holds the decision context fixed and re-queries the action after reversing one feature's sign. The resulting change belongs to the model--prompt--provider system under that intervention. It does not fully decompose the causes of the decision. Correlated features, interactions, unprobed inputs, response variability, and altered input plausibility can still affect the result. Our precision and recall therefore cover only the two probed features. The explanation--behavior gap is a behavioral index, not a report of hidden computation.

\paragraph{LLM agents in markets and strategic settings.}
LLM market simulations study financial behavior~\cite{chen2025}, and market games examine bounded strategic reasoning~\cite{evans2023}. Stylized facts are recurring statistical patterns in empirical returns~\cite{cont2001}. They can validate realistic finance simulations, whereas our market prioritizes controlled fundamental-return generation. Generative agents also operate in interactive social environments~\cite{park2023}. Related work addresses double-auction collusion~\cite{collusion2025}, strategic communication and information leakage~\cite{snake2026}, and adversarial coordination~\cite{byzantine2026}. We examine how institutional messages relate to declared reasons and measured decision sensitivity. We do not aim for trading realism or infer strategic intent.

\paragraph{Auditing, transparency, and accountability.}
Work on the limits of transparency~\cite{ananny2018}, legal debates about explanation~\cite{wachter2017}, and ethical frameworks for AI~\cite{floridi2018} warns against equating disclosure with understanding. Internal auditing frameworks~\cite{raji2020} and recursive audit architectures~\cite{triagent2025} provide institutional context. Cheap-talk theory~\cite{crawford1982} and narrative economics~\cite{shiller2019} help frame why messages may matter, although our study does not test those theories directly. The confirmatory phase compares feedback that names a driver with feedback that reports observational evidence. Neither kind is assumed to equal independent verification by a human institution.

\section{Materials and Methods}\label{sec:methods}
The confirmatory phase compares five conditions across three models (Figure~\ref{fig:design}). A seed fixes the random draws used in a run. The primary model is \texttt{deepseek-v4-flash}, with seeds 200--219. The replication models are \texttt{anthropic/claude-haiku-5.5} and \texttt{openai/gpt-6-luna}, with seeds 200--209 each. Every seed is crossed with all five conditions. Development uses only seeds 100--101 and is excluded from confirmation. We measure intervention-defined process alignment alongside environmental fidelity. The primary post-switch window is ticks 20--39, the fixed sensitivity window is 10--39, and all-tick summaries cover 0--39. These windows retain regime switches and stale messages. We did not choose a washout period after examining outcomes.

\subsection{Market environment and temporal ordering}
We use a synthetic market with one asset. In plain terms, each return combines the active driver, order imbalance, and noise, and the next price applies that return to the current price. Formally, let $g_t\in\{\mathrm{news},\mathrm{momentum}\}$ denote the active fundamental driver and $x_{g_t,t}$ its observed value. Requested signed sizes determine imbalance. The market updates according to
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 I_t&=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}s_{it}q_{it}}{5N},\qquad
 r_t=0.02x_{g_t,t}+0.03I_t+\epsilon_t,\\
 \epsilon_t&\sim\mathcal{N}(0,0.005^2),\qquad
 P_{t+1}=\max\{50,P_t(1+r_t)\},\quad P_0=100,
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:market}
\end{equation}
Here $s_{it}=+1$ for buy and $-1$ for sell, and $N=6$ in the confirmatory phase. The ground-truth driver controls the \emph{fundamental component} of returns. Aggregate orders and residual noise also affect realized returns. Momentum is generated as a signal rather than computed from past prices.

At each tick, an independent latent signal $z_t\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ enters the active feature with independent noise of standard deviation 0.7. The inactive news/momentum feature is independent noise with standard deviation 0.7. Volume is $1+0.3\mathcal{N}(0,1)$; spread is uniform on $[0.004,0.03]$; orderflow is $0.3\,\mathrm{momentum}+\mathcal{N}(0,0.6^2)$; sentiment is $0.3\,\mathrm{news}+\mathcal{N}(0,0.6^2)$. Distractors are features outside the fundamental term, although they can correlate with its driver. Features are stored at three decimals, except spread at four. Even seeds start in news and odd seeds in momentum. Regimes switch at ticks 10, 20, and 30.

Agents observe the current features and completed history before submitting orders. The market then computes the return and new price. After an audited failure in the confirmatory phase, a fine reduces cash and position alike before the selected trade settles. Both are multiplied by 0.90 at the post-step price. In intuitive terms, the fine applies to everything the agent owns, not only its cash. Formally, it equals 10\% of total marked wealth, $W=\mathrm{cash}+\mathrm{position}\times P_{t+1}$, even when cash alone is insufficient. Purchases require cash and sales require inventory; borrowing and short sales are not allowed. Requested sizes still enter imbalance. Re-asks never submit orders, change feedback, or advance history.

Paired seeds give every condition the same exogenous feature draws, residual shocks, and regime schedules for a given seed. Endogenous returns, price paths, portfolio states, and later observed return histories can still differ because agents act differently. Treatment effects include these feedback paths. Pairing does not make the complete market states identical.

\subsection{Agents, portfolios, and prompts}
The confirmatory phase crosses three fixed mandates (cautious, balanced, and growth) with two starting portfolios: cash USD 4000 and position 20, or cash USD 1000 and position 50. We use nominal USD to present the synthetic account units. Each agent begins at wealth USD 6000 at an initial asset price of USD 100. These simulated balances are separate from provider expenditures. Assignments remain identical across seeds, conditions, and models. Mandates change risk and sizing instructions. They do not create independent statistical replications or human-like personalities.

Each response must use strict JSON, a structured text format, with action buy/sell, integer size 1--10, and an ordered unique list of zero to two reasons drawn from momentum, spread, volume, news, orderflow, and sentiment. Extra keys, fabricated actions, coercion, and invalid-response retries are disallowed. There is no hold action. Reasoning/thinking output is disabled, and the maximum output length is 512 tokens. These rules constrain the response format but do not eliminate unobserved computation.

The system message contains the common instruction, institutional rules, and response schema. The user message contains the persona mandate, latest audit feedback if present, portfolio state, four completed feature--return pairs, and finally the current features. Current features use a fixed order and four-decimal formatting. The static prefix precedes dynamic material to facilitate prompt caching. We do not hypothesize a behavioral benefit from this ordering. All five rule paragraphs share the same true-driver framing and differ only in the final feedback-description sentence. Label and placebo have identical rule descriptions and disclosed-label wording. Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility} gives the complete verbatim prompts, feedback templates, rendered examples, response schema, and persona mandates.

\subsection{Feedback conditions}
An environmental audit checks whether the base reasons include the current fundamental driver. In all audited conditions, each decision has audit probability 0.30, and a failed audit incurs the total-wealth fine. The audit evaluates environmental fidelity rather than process precision. Both passing and failing audits supply feedback after the action. That feedback first appears in the next prompt and persists unchanged until the next audit, including across switches.

The placebo verdict remains truth-based, but its disclosed label is false at audit time. This intentionally conflicting message tests false authority. A persisted true label can become stale, and a persisted placebo label can become correct after a switch. We determine prompt exposure from logs rather than responses.

Evidence reports Pearson correlations, which summarize linear association, between each of all six features and realized returns over the last eight completed ticks. The window includes the audited tick, and features appear in fixed order. Feedback reports sample sizes and four-decimal coefficients. Fewer than two observations or zero variance yields ``unavailable''. The builder has no regime input, ranking, threshold, or preferred feature. Its associations include endogenous price impact and noise and can mix regimes. Evidence also summarizes a longer history than the four completed rows given to every agent. This longer history is part of the intervention, so evidence-versus-label is not a pure wording comparison.

\subsection{Preregistration, calibration, and amendments}\label{sec:amendments}
The original plan replaced features with adversarial extremes directed against the base action. Its pilot failed the ceiling gate: DeepSeek naive true-driver flips were 109/114 (0.956), above the 0.95 ceiling. The PI stopped that pilot. Amendment 1 prospectively replaced the instrument with sign reversal after calibration on dev seeds 100--101. Calibration compared zeroing, sign reversal, adverse shifts of one/two feature standard deviations, and the original extremes. The fixed rule required every model's pooled naive true-driver rate to lie in $[0.10,0.90]$. It maximized the minimum true-minus-non-driver discrimination across models, where discrimination is the difference between those flip rates. Differences within 0.02 were ties in a fixed perturbation order, and the best minimum had to be at least 0.15. Sign reversal was selected with minimum discrimination 0.184. This procedure calibrates a measurement instrument; it is not a confirmatory hypothesis test.

Independent dev null re-asks yielded naive flip rates of 0.043, 0.041, and 0.053 for DeepSeek, Haiku, and Luna. Net true-driver rates remained eligible, with minimum discrimination 0.197 in those separate noise runs. A common null rate cancels when subtracted from both true and non-driver rates before taking their difference. This subtraction does not remove all measurement error. Label outcomes did not enter the selection rule, but the PI and coordinator had seen descriptive dev label dependence exceeding naive. That exposure and the reuse of dev seeds for calibration and noise assessment limit the independence of development evidence. None of these observations is pooled with confirmation. The development reports retain and disclose extra dev null calls and quarantined overlapping-run artifacts.

Amendment 1 also added one null re-ask on a random 20\% of counterfactual-selected decisions, condition-specific noise monitoring, provider pins, and the temperature exceptions below. It removed the optional Qwen allocation on cost grounds. The four primary contrasts, H1--H5, directions, windows, aggregation, and inferential thresholds remained unchanged. The PI answered yes to all eight amended questions before confirmation.

Amendment 2 changed only budget operations after collection began. Higher Haiku costs prompted an account top-up and moved the OpenRouter procedural stop from USD 7.400 to USD 7.950, with the review reserve funded separately. The hard USD 8 provider cap remained in force, as did the DeepSeek USD 7.000 pause. No confirmatory outcome was inspected for that decision. The plan was refrozen with both amendments. Models, seeds, conditions, measurement, hypotheses, and analysis were unchanged. The renewed freeze commit is \texttt{72d05151df3fd1944fb009dda7506707ff455c82}.

\subsection{Counterfactual sign-flip instrument and noise monitoring}\label{sec:noise}
An independent Bernoulli(0.50) measurement mask randomly selects decisions before responses and is paired across conditions. For each valid selected base response, two re-asks retain its exact prompt state and settings. They replace only the current news or momentum value $v$ by $-v$. The intervention direction is independent of the base action, and zero remains zero without resampling. Portfolio, feedback, history, and other feature values remain fixed. We ignore counterfactual reasons. The probe measures whether this input intervention changes the action, including through a nonlinear response. It does not test trade-size explanations, necessary causation, or the other four features.

The amended design uses one byte-identical null re-ask on 20\% of sampled decisions to estimate response variability. Byte-identical means that every character and setting remains unchanged. Primary dependence and gap tests use raw flips. A secondary sensitivity subtracts the pooled model-by-condition null action-flip rate from pooled feature/driver rates, without clipping adjusted values to a fixed range. Pooling combines responses rather than averaging seed-level estimates. This adjustment does not redefine decision-level precision, recall, or SCI. The registered noise adjustment subtracts pooled all-tick model-by-condition null rates from pooled feature/driver rates. It is not a paired-seed adjustment of the primary contrasts or SCI. Raw flips therefore combine intervention sensitivity with re-ask variability. Null-rate intervals are descriptive Wilson intervals over responses and do not account for seed clustering. Section~\ref{sec:protocol} reports deviations in realization of the null mask.

\subsection{Metrics and estimands}\label{sec:metrics}
In intuitive terms, we compare what the agent cites with what changes its action under the probe. Indicator notation records whether a condition holds. For decision $j$, let $a_j$ be the base action, $a_j^{(f)}$ its action after feature $f$ is sign-reversed, $R_j$ the base reasons, and $g_j$ the current true fundamental driver. With $\Fprobe=\{\mathrm{news},\mathrm{momentum}\}$, define
\begin{equation}
 E_j=\ind[g_j\in R_j],\qquad d_{jf}=\ind[a_j^{(f)}\ne a_j],\qquad
 C_j=R_j\cap\Fprobe,\qquad D_j=\{f\in\Fprobe:d_{jf}=1\}.
\end{equation}
Environmental fidelity $E_j$ uses valid base responses. True-driver dependence is $d_{j,g_j}$ and non-driver dependence is $d_{j,\bar g_j}$, where $\bar g_j$ denotes the other feature. Process precision asks how many cited probe features also change the action when reversed. Process recall asks how many action-changing probe features were cited. The unconditional alignment score compares the complete citation and dependence sets. For complete valid measurements,
\begin{equation}
 P_j=\frac{|C_j\cap D_j|}{|C_j|}\ (|C_j|>0),\qquad
 Q_j=\frac{|C_j\cap D_j|}{|D_j|}\ (|D_j|>0),\qquad
 U_j=1-\frac{|C_j\triangle D_j|}{2}.
 \label{eq:process}
\end{equation}
Precision $P_j$ and recall $Q_j$ measure citation agreement with this particular sign-flip probe, not ground-truth process accuracy. They remain undefined at zero denominators and apply only within $\Fprobe$. An unprobed distractor citation is not declared a false positive. The released analysis reports structural undefined scores separately from parse/transport failures, together with citation coverage and dependence-set sizes. The secondary unconditional alignment score $U_j$ counts two empty sets as agreement and therefore answers a different question.

If the actual prompt contains label $L_j$, adoption is $A_j=\ind[L_j\in R_j]$, conditional on exposure. We report cited/exposed counts and proportions separately for currently correct and stale labels. Intuitively, SCI asks whether a citation changes more or less than the corresponding sign-flip dependence. For matched eligible measured coordinates $J_{sc}$ in seed $s$, condition $c$, and naive reference $n$, define the strategic-compliance index (SCI), or explanation--behavior gap, by
\begin{align}
 \mathrm{SCI}_s(c;X)
 &=\frac{1}{|J_{sc}|}\sum_{j\in J_{sc}}
 \left(\ind[X_j\in R_{cj}]-\ind[X_j\in R_{nj}]\right)\nonumber\\
 &\quad-\frac{1}{|J_{sc}|}\sum_{j\in J_{sc}}
 \left(d_{cj,X_j}-d_{nj,X_j}\right).
 \label{eq:sci}
\end{align}
SCI is an operational difference between citation change and sign-flip-dependence change. Its scale and zero point are not externally validated measures of explanation completeness, concealment, or intent.

Primary label SCI selects $X_j=g_j$ and primary placebo SCI selects $X_j=\bar g_j$ at every primary-window coordinate, including before exposure. These intention-to-treat (ITT) selectors include coordinates regardless of whether an audit occurred, avoiding selection on treatment-dependent audits. A positive SCI means citation increased more than measured dependence. A negative SCI means dependence increased more. Neither sign establishes a motive, deliberate strategy, or deception. Secondary exposure SCI instead applies the actual possibly stale $L_{cj}$ to both paired prompts.

Within each seed, process scores average defined decision-level values rather than pooled numerator/denominator ratios. Contrasts use common tick/agent coordinates with valid base responses and both counterfactuals in both conditions. Precision contrasts also require a defined score in both conditions. This restriction means precision's estimand can depend on treatment. Each eligible seed receives equal weight. We report all-available condition means separately; they need not equal the means underlying a contrast or SCI component.

\subsection{Hypotheses and statistical plan}\label{sec:statistics}
The confirmatory-phase hypotheses remain as registered. H1 predicts positive evidence-minus-naive gains in both precision and true-driver dependence. Evidence's dependence advantage over punitive is secondary. H2 predicts greater environmental fidelity/true-driver citation under label, a positive label precision contrast, and a larger precision gain under evidence than label. The precision comparisons are secondary. H3 predicts a positive label SCI. H4 predicts a positive placebo SCI, increased false citation, and lower environmental fidelity than label. H5 predicts a smaller precision gain under punitive than evidence. A nonsignificant punitive comparison cannot establish equivalence. P2 compares the complete evidence audit package with naive, and P4 compares the placebo audit package with naive. Neither isolates numerical content or label falsity.

The sole DeepSeek primary family contains four two-sided paired $t$ tests of equal-seed differences: P1 evidence-minus-naive precision, P2 evidence-minus-naive true-driver dependence, P3 label SCI, and P4 placebo SCI. A paired test compares conditions within the same seed. The family uses $\alpha=0.05$ with four-slot Holm step-down correction regardless of effect sign. Holm correction adjusts $p$ values sequentially to account for multiple comparisons. Missing/untestable contrasts retain $p=1$. A two-sided rejection opposite to prediction does not support a positive hypothesis. Each replication uses its own four-slot correction, without pooling models or correcting across models. Replications cannot rescue a failed primary prediction. H2/H5 and the secondary components of H4 have no additional confirmatory rejection rule.

The released analysis reports paired differences, paired SD and $d_z=\bar\Delta/\mathrm{SD}(\Delta_s)$, sample sizes, and raw/adjusted $p$ values. Paired SD is the standard deviation of seed-level differences. Descriptive 95\% intervals come from 10,000 paired-seed percentile bootstrap resamples with RNG seed 20261026. Bootstrapping repeatedly resamples seeds to describe uncertainty. These intervals are not simultaneous confidence intervals and do not override the registered $t$ test. The prospective standardized minimum detectable effects at 80\% power and the conservative first Holm threshold 0.0125 were $d_z=0.8146$ for 20 seeds and 1.2843 for 10. Small effects can be missed. More agents or re-asks do not increase the replication count.

Registered sensitivities include all paired-seed sign permutations and decision-level regressions with condition, tick, regime, persona, initial inventory, and seed fixed effects. Fixed effects allow separate intercepts for each category. Standard errors cluster by market seed across all conditions, using CR1 finite-cluster correction and $G-1$ reference degrees of freedom. A null-imposed Rademacher wild bootstrap uses 9,999 draws. These diagnostic tests do not create a replacement primary family. Pooled Gaussian run-mean mixed models include condition, model, and their interaction, with seed and model-by-seed random intercepts, as secondary descriptions. Wilcoxon tests and Cohen's $h$ in the released analysis are exploratory supplements absent from the frozen plan and do not determine verdicts.

All assigned runs remain in the manifest. Primary pairs require complete 40-tick runs and at least one eligible common coordinate. Invalid JSON and exhausted transport retries count as missing; we never fabricate actions. A failed base yields no order. The market advances with available orders and the fixed six-agent denominator. Valid bases with incomplete counterfactuals retain environmental/citation scores but are excluded from complete-case process scores. Missing nulls do not invalidate otherwise valid process measurements. Structural undefined scores remain distinct. The analysis reports worst/best missing-score bounds for unconditional alignment and feature dependence. It also reports denominators and missingness by run, feature, and exposure.

\subsection{Models, transport, and execution safeguards}\label{sec:transport}
DeepSeek used the direct DeepSeek chat-completions transport at temperature zero. Haiku and Luna used OpenRouter pinned respectively to \texttt{anthropic} and \texttt{openai}, with \texttt{allow\_fallbacks:false} and \texttt{data\_collection:deny}. We omitted temperature for both replications because the endpoint listings checked on 27 October 2026 did not advertise support. They therefore used provider-default sampling. The original universal temperature-zero plan does not describe these routes. We logged the returned serving-provider identity. A missing/mismatched provider would be a charged failed call, but no provider mismatch was observed. Model IDs and access dates identify the deployed services, not immutable model weights.

The transport enforced JSON, disabled reasoning/thinking, and logged usage and retries. It allowed at most three identical-payload attempts for transient failures, with delays of one then two seconds. We did not resample valid responses for a preferred action. During development, we evaluated and rejected a free OpenCode CLI route because it added runtime system text, quoted the user payload, and generated title requests. It could not preserve the experimental messages. No OpenCode output enters either confirmatory model cohort.

Independent audit and counterfactual RNG streams use seeds $1000s+7$ and $1000s+17$. RNG means random number generator. The amended null recipe specified a full independent mask from $1000s+29$. Audit masks are paired across audited conditions and counterfactual masks across all conditions. Conditions rotate in order by seed, and all conditions of a seed precede the next seed. Exclusive block locks, bounded concurrency, and a guard validating the freeze commit/hash protect collection. Only the coordinator resumes interrupted blocks. There was no outcome-based stopping or replacement-seed rule. The repository retains code, raw prompts, model/provider metadata, masks, and per-call accounting.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}
\subsection{Confirmatory phase (preregistered): completion and primary results}\label{sec:primary}
The confirmatory runs were complete, and every DeepSeek primary estimate opposed its predicted positive direction. All 200 assigned runs were included: 100 DeepSeek runs and 50 each for Haiku and Luna. Every primary contrast retained all 20 or 10 seed pairs. Invalid base decisions across all conditions/ticks numbered 0 for DeepSeek, 6 for Haiku, and 3 for Luna. Missingness does not conceal an incomplete condition or replaced seed (Appendix~\ref{app:coverage}).

Table~\ref{tab:primary} reports all four primary contrasts for all models, and Figure~\ref{fig:forest} displays their intervals. In DeepSeek, P1 evidence-minus-naive precision was $-0.0137$ with an interval spanning zero. P3 label SCI was $-0.0389$ and also inconclusive. Neither result establishes a zero effect. \subsection{Independent model replications}
The replication models provide no corrected support for a predicted positive primary effect. Haiku had no Holm-significant primary contrast. Its evidence precision estimate was positive ($+0.0163$). Evidence dependence ($-0.0059$), label SCI ($-0.0354$), and placebo SCI ($-0.0572$) were negative and inconclusive under the registered tests. \section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
\subsection{What an auditor's agreement establishes}
Agreement with an auditor does not show what controlled an action. Environmental fidelity alone cannot establish that relationship. Under the registered sign-reversal instrument, the label and placebo audit packages change measured sign-flip sensitivity as well as citations relative to naive. Correct-label SCI provides no evidence of disproportionate citation improvement in any model. Inconclusive tests cannot exclude all explanation-only changes. The principal placebo-package result is narrower. DeepSeek's negative SCI indicates that the increase in opposite-driver sign-flip dependence exceeded the increase in opposite-driver citation by 0.1341 under the registered SCI. The gap is defined relative to naive. It does not mean that explanations never mention the false feature.

Oversight should assess environmental agreement and behavioral alignment separately. An auditor can change the system it evaluates, so its message is an intervention as well as a measurement. Audits should test message accuracy, including stale and incorrect feedback, alongside whether the agent repeats the correct answer. The Article 13 motivation is methodological: information supplied for interpretation should have an evidential basis. This experiment does not establish a legal sufficiency test or a complete operational audit standard.

\subsection{False-authority risk}
The placebo audit package can change decisions and citations, but this experiment cannot isolate why. The placebo intervention combines an environmental verdict with a conflicting authoritative driver label. Its effects could reflect message salience, ambiguity resolution, or reliance on the disclosed feature. We cannot isolate those mechanisms. Under the registered SCI, the placebo package increased opposite-driver sign-flip dependence more than citation relative to naive in DeepSeek. These two response rates are not calibrated measurements of a common latent quantity. Salience, response variability, fixed-history inconsistency or citation-format constraints could contribute. The result motivates testing responses to erroneous authority. It does not establish under-explanation, concealment or a mechanism attributable to label falsity alone.

The model differences constrain this result. Haiku's placebo estimate is negative but fails its corrected primary test. Luna's is positive and inconclusive. The DeepSeek result therefore does not establish how LLMs generally respond to audits. Persisted labels and the deliberate verdict/label inconsistency also differ from many practical audits. These features make false authority testable here while limiting direct deployment generalization.

\subsection{Why numerical evidence may hurt}
The evidence audit package did not yield the predicted gains in registered process measures. The registered evidence prediction failed. The evidence condition had lower true-driver dependence than naive in DeepSeek and Luna. The registered comparison does not isolate the effect of its correlation table from audits and fines. Luna precision was also lower, while Haiku remained inconclusive. Instrument checks passed on the pooled flip-rate gate only. Precision/recall measure agreement with the probe rather than ground-truth process accuracy. Exploratory evidence-minus-punitive dependence estimates remain negative, but their intervals are descriptive and the history/content difference is not isolated. Several explanations remain compatible with the design. Short-window correlations can be noisy, the evidence window can span a regime switch, and observed returns contain endogenous order imbalance. A table over every feature may distribute attention toward distractors or preserve an outdated association. Its longer summarized history also changes information availability rather than presentation alone. Future factorial tests could examine these interpretations; they are not established mediators.

Lower dependence does not automatically imply poorer economic performance. The true feature governs only the fundamental return component, and appropriate trading can also depend on portfolio constraints or impact. The observed decreases concern the registered sign-flip-sensitivity/precision targets in this setting. Reversed or absent discrimination precludes describing every decrease as poorer driver use. Evidence has a relative precision advantage over punitive in DeepSeek, but it does not show an advantage over naive.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}
The market is synthetic and deliberately simple. News/momentum are generated variables, and a known switch schedule governs the fundamental component. Forced buy/sell choices can elicit decisions when evidence is weak. Short horizons and an oracle environmental auditor that knows the true driver limit resemblance to real markets. Treatment changes market paths through agent orders. A paired seed controls exogenous draws but does not isolate a direct linguistic mechanism. We tested only three deployed models and one prompt family, without immutable checkpoints or broad prompt/order counterbalancing.

The instrument probes only news and momentum, holds history and correlated distractors fixed, and records action rather than size. Sign reversal avoids the original adversarial extremes but can still produce an unusual joint feature/context combination. The probe can miss interactions, asymmetric thresholds, correlated predictors, and altered confidence without an action change. Sensitivity does not show that a feature was necessary or capture complete process faithfulness. Passing the pooled flip-rate gate does not remedy the news-regime discrimination reversal in DeepSeek or near-zero discrimination in Haiku. Review-requested strata preserve the negative DeepSeek P2/P4 directions in both regimes but reveal opposing Luna P4 signs. These exploratory results narrow the interpretation rather than validate the instrument. We therefore cannot interpret every flip as correct use of the environmental driver.

Null re-asks reveal response variability, including with DeepSeek temperature zero. Haiku and Luna used provider-default sampling without temperature control. Rate subtraction is a secondary additive sensitivity. It does not correct individual decisions or statistically separate signal from noise through deconvolution. The new paired sensitivity resamples seed-level rates and contrasts together. All-tick cell subtraction cannot eliminate time/regime heterogeneity or estimate a pure feature effect. Model-by-condition noise differences and the disclosed null-mask/invalid-base deviations complicate that interpretation. Provider-default replication sampling also prevents attributing cross-model differences to model identity alone. Precision and recall have treatment-dependent undefined denominators. Common support reduces comparability problems without making the resulting estimand unconditional. The gap can reflect either component. Its sign does not identify internal reasoning or intent, and it cannot establish deception.

Development calibration and noise checks reused dev seeds, and the PI/coordinator saw descriptive label outcomes before freezing. The amendments preserve the original plan and hypotheses transparently. They do not erase that exposure or make instrument choice independent of the development context. The second amendment occurred during collection and was restricted to costs and operations. Appendix~\ref{app:preliminary} gives additional preliminary-phase limitations. Future preregistered work should validate instruments across regimes and prompt families before testing treatment effects.

Fixed personas and inventory differences do not make six independent decision makers. Within-run dependence remains. Seed counts limit sensitivity to small effects, and the two replications have fewer seeds than the primary model cohort. An inconclusive contrast is not equivalence. A positive or negative SCI is an operational behavioral comparison, not evidence for a human motivational category.

\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}
The confirmatory experiment shows why explanation change and decision change must be measured separately. Beyond the exploratory motivation (Appendix~\ref{app:preliminary}), all four primary DeepSeek estimates oppose their predicted positive directions. The evidence-dependence effect ($\Delta=-0.0624$, Holm $p=0.0140$) and placebo SCI ($-0.1341$, Holm $p=4.18\times10^{-5}$) reject after correction. Evidence precision and label SCI remain inconclusive.

The evidence condition also reduces true-driver sign-flip dependence relative to naive in Luna ($\Delta=-0.0909$, Holm $p=0.0376$), while Haiku has no corrected primary rejection. This estimates an audit package, not isolated numerical content. In DeepSeek's placebo condition, the increase in opposite-driver sign-flip dependence exceeded the increase in opposite-driver citation by 0.1341 under the registered SCI. This is also a package contrast. The negative gap does not generalize across both replications or establish explanation incompleteness or deceptive intent. Exploratory regime and paired noise analyses preserve the main DeepSeek directions but reveal heterogeneous Luna SCI and sensitivity of label SCI to noise adjustment. No registered conclusion is replaced. Auditor agreement is insufficient evidence of process alignment, and the evidence package is not automatically beneficial. Evaluation should distinguish environmental agreement, intervention-defined dependence, coverage, noise, and incorrect-authority responses while respecting the limits of the probe.

\section{Preliminary exploratory phase}\label{app:preliminary}
The earlier exploratory phase used eight conditions, eight agents per run, 40 ticks, and 10 seeds per condition: 80 runs and 25,600 decisions. Its conditions were control (\texttt{ctrl}, no explanation mandate), naive mandate (\texttt{naive}, the baseline for all reported contrasts), punitive audit, corrective audit, reputation with corrective feedback, audit plus reputation, placebo audit, and reputation-only. DeepSeek V4 Flash ran at temperature zero with identical initial states, producing near-deterministic agents. We measured only environmental fidelity and did not execute the planned counterfactual process attribution. Development and evaluation seeds overlapped. Hypotheses were revised after pilot evidence, and the planned audit-rate comparison became a feedback-content comparison. The v1 history defect in \texttt{src/market.py} yielded only three completed feature--return rows instead of the intended four. History was extracted before stepping after adding the current feature.

The preliminary phase's primary-window run-level environmental fidelity was 0.627 under naive, 0.660 under punitive audit, 0.750 under corrective audit, 0.751 under reputation with corrective feedback, 0.745 under audit plus reputation, 0.667 under reputation-only, and 0.587 under placebo. The corrective-minus-naive difference was $+0.123$ (paired $p=0.0068$, Holm $p=0.0259$; 95\% seed-bootstrap CI [0.056, 0.186]). Punitive audit was inconclusive ($\Delta=+0.033$, $p=0.351$, CI [$-0.027$, 0.095]). These post hoc contrasts supply no confirmatory evidence.

Label adoption was the main diagnostic. Environmental fidelity was 1.000 when the prompt contained a currently correct revealed label. The probability of citing a revealed label was 1.000 even for false labels. Excluding all prompts with disclosed driver labels reduced the corrective contrast to $+0.052$ ($p=0.258$; Holm $p=0.516$; CI [$-0.031$, 0.129]). Placebo was below corrective ($p=0.0004$), but its contrast with naive was inconclusive ($p=0.318$). The environmental gain is consistent with auditor echo. It does not establish independent driver recovery or explain what controlled the actions.

Several design features limit interpretation: condition-specific true-driver wording, reputation-dependent order-size multipliers, fines implemented on cash despite wealth wording, nearly identical starting agents, and development/evaluation overlap.
