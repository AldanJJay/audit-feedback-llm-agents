\abstract{Can audit feedback change what large language model (LLM) trading agents cite and how they act? Oversight relies on stated reasons; feedback that changes citations without decisions could make them unreliable audit evidence. We compare five feedback packages, from no audit (naive) to verdicts with correct labels, return evidence or false labels, in a preregistered market alternating news and momentum drivers (20 paired DeepSeek seeds; 10 each for Haiku and Luna). Sign-flip sensitivity measures how often actions change under feature reversal. Relative to naive, evidence reduced DeepSeek's true-driver sign-flip sensitivity (\texorpdfstring{$\Delta=-0.0624$}{Delta=-0.0624}, Holm \texorpdfstring{$p=0.0140$}{p=0.0140}, four tests per model). False-label pairs a truth-based verdict and fine with a contradictory, persistent label. DeepSeek's citation--sensitivity difference (CSD), citation change minus sensitivity change relative to naive, was negative (\texorpdfstring{$-0.1341$}{-0.1341}, Holm \texorpdfstring{$p<0.001$}{p<0.001}). This result was not statistically confirmed in the replications' corrected tests. Luna showed lower evidence precision (\texorpdfstring{$-0.1198$}{-0.1198}, Holm \texorpdfstring{$p=0.0007$}{p=0.0007}) and true-driver sensitivity (\texorpdfstring{$-0.0909$}{-0.0909}, Holm \texorpdfstring{$p=0.0376$}{p=0.0376}); Haiku had no significant contrasts after Holm correction. Failed news-regime discrimination for DeepSeek and Haiku limits interpretation of these assigned-package effects. We report separate citation and action measurements and workload for a networked audit service.}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
An audit can change the actions and cited reasons of a large language model (LLM) agent trading through an application programming interface (API). Rationales may omit influences~\cite{turpin2023}, and self-generated counterfactual accounts may misdescribe decision boundaries~\cite{wang2025}.

Oversight relies on stated reasons to assess whether an agent responds to relevant information. Internal auditing frameworks organize accountability procedures~\cite{raji2020}, while multi-LLM architectures incorporate transparency audits~\cite{triagent2025}; access to explanations alone does not establish what they reveal about decisions~\cite{ananny2018}. If audit feedback teaches an agent to cite the feature the auditor rewards while leaving its decisions unchanged, those citations lose value as audit evidence. The question is whether adopting an auditor's label changes the decision or only the explanation; answering it requires measuring both responses.

We compare five assigned packages in a preregistered synthetic market whose fundamental-return driver switches between news and momentum. Naive disables audits; punitive supplies a verdict and fine; label adds the audited tick's true driver; evidence adds return correlations; and false-label adds the opposite driver. \emph{Environmental fidelity} records citation of the current driver, whereas \emph{restricted process alignment} measures precision and recall between citations and the features whose sign reversal changes trading direction within a two-feature probe set. In an earlier exploratory phase, agents adopted the auditor's label in their stated reasons, raising the question of whether their decisions changed too; all confirmatory data come from a separate paired design. We also estimate workload for an audit sidecar that probes decisions separately from trade execution.

We contribute a preregistered paired design for comparing assigned feedback packages; separate measurement of citation and sign-flip sensitivity, with the citation--sensitivity difference (CSD) defined as citation change minus sensitivity change relative to naive; per-regime instrument validation identifying where the probe fails to discriminate the driver; and request, token and cost measurements for a networked audit sidecar.

\subsection{Background and related work}\label{sec:related}
Interpretability research distinguishes model transparency from post hoc explanation~\cite{rudin2019,lipton2018}. Surveys organize explanation methods~\cite{guidotti2018,samek2021,barredo2020}, while social and pragmatic accounts relate explanations to recipients' needs~\cite{miller2019,paez2019}. Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations (LIME)~\cite{ribeiro2016} and SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP)~\cite{lundberg2017} assign feature importance to predictions. Evaluating agents' own reasons also requires distinguishing faithfulness from plausibility~\cite{jacovi2020}, by checking whether they correspond to behaviour under the model, prompt and provider configuration used. A position paper on LLM accounts of human decisions argues that explanatory credit requires evidence beyond accurate prediction or plausible rationales~\cite{lee2026}.

Market simulations examine how interacting agents produce market behaviour~\cite{chen2025,evans2023}, with empirical return regularities providing a reference for financial modelling~\cite{cont2001}. Generative-agent simulations~\cite{park2023} and evaluations of collusion~\cite{collusion2025}, strategic communication~\cite{snake2026} and coordination~\cite{byzantine2026} examine interactions among LLM agents. Strategic information transmission~\cite{crawford1982} and narrative economics~\cite{shiller2019} also treat messages as influences on behaviour. Audit feedback extends this question to messages about agents' explanations.

Debates over explanation rights~\cite{wachter2017} concern the information available to people affected by automated decisions, while ethical frameworks~\cite{floridi2018} place explicability alongside other requirements for responsible AI. Article 13 of the European Union's Artificial Intelligence (AI) Act requires transparency and information for high-risk AI systems that enable deployers to interpret their output and use it appropriately~\cite{ai_act_2024}. Our experiment addresses the narrower measurement gap between feedback-induced changes in citations and action sensitivity under a controlled perturbation; it does not assess legal compliance.

\section{Materials and Methods}\label{sec:methods}
\subsection{Market, agents and paired design}\label{sec:dgp}
Six agents trade for 40 ticks per run, using 20 DeepSeek seeds (200--219) and 10 each for Haiku and Luna (200--209) per package; development seeds 100--101 are excluded. Pairing fixes exogenous draws across packages, while orders determine trading histories. Even seeds begin in news and odd seeds in momentum, switching at ticks 10, 20 and 30. The inclusive windows are ticks 20--39 for primary outcomes, 10--39 for sensitivity checks and 0--39 for all-tick summaries (Figure~\ref{fig:design}).

\begin{figure}[htbp]\centering
\includegraphics[width=.92\linewidth]{figures/fig1_design.pdf}
\caption{Design and eligibility. Naive disables audits; punitive supplies a verdict and fine; label adds the audited tick's true driver; evidence adds return correlations; false-label adds the opposite driver (Table~\ref{tab:conditions}). Only base actions trade. Complete measurement requires a valid base and both valid sign-flip probes; precision also requires cited probe features, and recall requires action-changing probe features. The citation--sensitivity difference (CSD) is citation change minus sign-flip-rate change relative to naive. Matched decisions in ticks 20--39 yield equal-seed contrasts, with four-test Holm correction per model. Identical-prompt null re-asks diagnose variability.}\label{fig:design}\end{figure}

Let $t$ index ticks and $g_t\in\{\mathrm{news},\mathrm{momentum}\}$ denote the active fundamental-return driver. The displayed features are momentum $M_t$, news $N_t$, volume $V_t$, spread $B_t$, orderflow $O_t$ and sentiment $S_t$. A star denotes an unrounded parent; $\operatorname{round}_k$ rounds to $k$ decimal places, and $\ind[\cdot]$ equals one when its condition holds and zero otherwise. The random inputs are independent: latent signal $z_t\sim N(0,1)$, volume innovation $v_t\sim N(0,1)$, momentum and news noises $e_{mt},e_{nt}\sim N(0,0.7^2)$, orderflow and sentiment innovations $u_{ot},u_{st}\sim N(0,0.6^2)$, and raw spread $b_t\sim U(0.004,0.03)$. They generate
\begin{align}
 M_t^*&=\ind[g_t=\mathrm{momentum}]z_t+e_{mt},&N_t^*&=\ind[g_t=\mathrm{news}]z_t+e_{nt},\nonumber\\
 M_t&=\operatorname{round}_3(M_t^*),&N_t&=\operatorname{round}_3(N_t^*),\nonumber\\
 V_t&=\operatorname{round}_3(1+0.3v_t),& B_t&=\operatorname{round}_4(b_t),\nonumber\\
 O_t&=\operatorname{round}_3(0.3M_t^*+u_{ot}),&S_t&=\operatorname{round}_3(0.3N_t^*+u_{st}).\label{eq:dgp}
\end{align}
Volume is untruncated, while orderflow and sentiment proxy the unrounded momentum and news inputs, with parent correlations of 0.5210 when active and 0.3304 when inactive.

Orders affect returns through requested-order imbalance $I_t$, normalized by $6\times5=30$. For agent $i$ at tick $t$, let $h_{it}=+1$ for buy and $-1$ for sell, and let requested size be $q_{it}\in\{1,\ldots,10\}$. The active displayed value $x_{g_t,t}$ equals $M_t$ in momentum and $N_t$ in news. With pre-floor market return $r_t$, independent return noise $\eta_t$, and price $\pi_t$ initially $\pi_0=100$, the update is
\begin{equation}
 I_t=\frac{\sum_{i:\mathrm{valid}}h_{it}q_{it}}{30},\quad r_t=0.02x_{g_t,t}+0.03I_t+\eta_t,\quad
 \eta_t\sim N(0,0.005^2),\quad \pi_{t+1}=\max\{50,\pi_t(1+r_t)\}.\label{eq:market}
\end{equation}
Requested orders enter imbalance before cash and inventory settlement caps, and invalid bases place no order. Returns are logged before the price floor. Drawing $z_t,e_{mt},e_{nt},v_t,u_{ot},u_{st},b_t$ in that order, then return noise after decisions, preserves the stream across packages.

The agents pair each of three mandates (cautious, balanced and growth) with two initial portfolios: cash 4000 and position 20, or cash 1000 and position 50. Both start at wealth 6000, with no hold action, borrowing or short selling. Prompts contain the mandate, portfolio, latest feedback, four completed feature--return rows and current features. A strict JavaScript Object Notation (JSON) schema requires direction, size and at most two reasons. Appendix~\ref{app:prompts} gives the literal prompts.

\subsection{Assigned audit packages and exposure}\label{sec:packages}
Table~\ref{tab:conditions} distinguishes each package's feedback. All audited packages use the same rule: each decision is audited with probability 0.30 and passes if it cites the current true driver. Citing both drivers therefore guarantees a pass. Failure multiplies cash and position by 0.9 before settlement, imposing a fine equal to 10\% of post-step wealth.

Package assignment precedes decisions; feedback first arrives in a later prompt and persists until the next audit. A regime switch can therefore make a true-at-audit label false or a false-at-audit label true. Primary intention-to-treat comparisons use the current driver or its opposite for every eligible assigned decision, regardless of the communicated label. Comparisons with punitive share the verdict and fine policy, although portfolios and histories can diverge (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

The evidence package delivered fewer observations than registered. At audit tick $a$, the builder received four history rows plus the just-completed tick, covering $k=\max(0,a-4),\ldots,a$. Every primary-window table used five observations and persisted until the next audit; none reached the registered eight observations.

\subsection{Sign-flip instrument, metrics and eligibility}\label{sec:instrument}
The probe tests whether reversing a feature changes trading direction. A pre-treatment mask selects decisions with probability 0.50. After a valid base response, separate re-asks reverse news and then momentum. For each targeted feature $f$, the re-ask replaces its displayed value $x_{f,t}$ with $-x_{f,t}$, using four decimal places and leaving zero unchanged. All other context stays fixed, and neither re-ask trades.

Let $s$ index seeds, $c$ conditions, and $j$ the decision coordinate comprising seed, tick $t$ and agent $i$. The current driver is $g_j=g_t$ at that tick, and $\neg g_j$ denotes the other member of the probe set $\Fprobe=\{\mathrm{news},\mathrm{momentum}\}$. For cited reasons $R_j$, define the cited probe set $C_j=R_j\cap\Fprobe$. Let $a_j$ be the base direction and $a_j^{(f)}$ the direction after reversing feature $f$. Define $d_{jf}$ as one for a direction change and zero otherwise; size changes alone leave it zero. The detected set is $D_j=\{f\in\Fprobe:d_{jf}=1\}$. Environmental fidelity $E_j$, process precision $P_j$ and process recall $Q_j$ are
\begin{equation}
 E_j=\ind[g_j\in R_j],\quad d_{jf}=\ind[a_j^{(f)}\ne a_j],\quad
 P_j=\frac{|C_j\cap D_j|}{|C_j|},\quad Q_j=\frac{|C_j\cap D_j|}{|D_j|}.\label{eq:metrics}
\end{equation}
Precision $P$ and recall $Q$ average only defined scores, excluding observations with empty denominators. Environmental fidelity $E$ is the true-driver citation rate. The means of $d_{j,g_j}$ and $d_{j,\neg g_j}$, denoted $d_g$ and $d_{\neg g}$, are true-driver and opposite-driver sign-flip sensitivity.

Comparisons use jointly eligible coordinates. Write $b^{\mathrm{valid}}_{cj}$ for valid-base status and $b^{\mathrm{complete}}_{cj}$ for a valid base with both valid probes. Eligibility $A^Y_{cj}$ is $b^{\mathrm{valid}}_{cj}$ for citation and fidelity, $b^{\mathrm{complete}}_{cj}$ for sensitivity, $b^{\mathrm{complete}}_{cj}\ind[|C_{cj}|>0]$ for precision, and $b^{\mathrm{complete}}_{cj}\ind[|D_{cj}|>0]$ for recall. For target arm $c$ and reference arm $r$, $J_{sc}^{Y}$ contains matched eligible coordinates within seed $s$, and $n_s$ counts eligible seed pairs:
\begin{equation}
 J_{sc}^{Y}=\{j:20\le t\le39,\ A_{cj}^{Y}=A_{rj}^{Y}=1\},\quad
 \Delta_s^Y=|J_{sc}^{Y}|^{-1}\!\sum_{j\in J_{sc}^{Y}}(Y_{cj}-Y_{rj}),\quad
 \Delta^Y=n_s^{-1}\sum_s\Delta_s^Y.\label{eq:support}
\end{equation}
Seeds receive equal weight. Tables suppress the metric superscript on $\Delta$ and report $n_j$, the total matched decisions per arm across seeds.

The \emph{citation--sensitivity difference} (CSD) subtracts the sensitivity change from the citation change relative to naive. Let $X_j$ select a target feature, let $\mathrm{nv}$ denote the naive reference arm, and define $J_{sc}$ as the coordinates with valid bases and both valid probes in $c$ and naive during ticks 20--39. Its seed contrast is
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{CSD}_s(c;X)=\frac{1}{|J_{sc}|}\sum_{j\in J_{sc}}\left[\ind[X_j\in R_{cj}]-\ind[X_j\in R_{\mathrm{nv},j}]-(d_{cj,X_j}-d_{\mathrm{nv},j,X_j})\right].\label{eq:csd}
\end{equation}
For CSD, $\Delta$ directly averages these seed contrasts, so positive values indicate that citation increased more than sensitivity. The components share complete-probe support and preserve paired covariance. Actual-label secondary scores require exposure in the target arm and evaluate both arms against that communicated label.

Invalid or failed responses are excluded without imputation or resampling. Missing null re-asks leave raw primary observations eligible. Section~\ref{sec:secondary} reports coverage, and Section~\ref{sec:limitations} discusses the selection induced by these eligibility rules.

\subsection{Hypotheses, analysis status and reproducibility}\label{sec:analysis}
The frozen plan distinguishes primary, secondary and sensitivity analyses. Amendment 1 replaced a saturating probe with sign reversal; Amendment 2 changed only the budget. All primary predictions concern positive contrasts in ticks 20--39:
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
\item H1 predicts positive evidence minus naive precision (P1) and true-driver sensitivity (P2). Both require matched complete probes; P1 also requires nonempty cited probe sets in both arms. Positive evidence minus punitive sensitivity is H1's registered secondary prediction.
\item H2 predicts positive label minus naive fidelity and precision, and evidence minus label precision, all secondary. Fidelity equals current true-driver citation.
\item H3 predicts positive label CSD relative to naive (P3), selecting the current driver on matched complete probes.
\item H4 predicts positive false-label CSD relative to naive (P4), selecting the opposite current driver on matched complete probes, plus greater false-label adoption and lower fidelity than label. Table~\ref{tab:secondary}'s current-opposite citation differs from actual communicated-label adoption among prompts exposed to a currently false label (public repository, Data Availability Statement).
\item H5 predicts higher evidence precision than punitive, a secondary test with no primary counterpart. Punitive minus naive precision supplies context; nonsignificance cannot establish equivalence.
\end{itemize}

Primary inference uses two-sided paired-$t$ tests across seeds, with Holm step-down correction across P1--P4 within each model at $\alpha=0.05$. The paired standardized effect size is $d_z=\Delta/\operatorname{SD}(\Delta_s)$, where SD denotes sample standard deviation. Descriptive 95\% confidence intervals (CIs) use paired-seed bootstraps and do not provide simultaneous coverage. Registered secondary outcomes comprise citation, fidelity, recall, alignment, label adoption and exposure, punitive comparisons and regime summaries. The registered sensitivity checks assess the primary contrasts using the wider window, seed sign-flip permutations, seed-clustered regressions, wild bootstrap and additive null-rate adjustment. Table~\ref{tab:secondary} identifies each row's status. Component decomposition, correctness-stratified CSD, label--punitive precision, null-condition tests, fixed-path correlation and directionality checks, and traffic summaries are post hoc analyses.

Null re-asks repeat the unchanged base prompt to measure response variability. Monitoring departed from the registered 20\% full-mask procedure and covered approximately 18.5--18.6\% of probed decisions; feature-probe masks were unaffected. The requested identifiers were \texttt{deepseek-v4-flash} via the native API for DeepSeek, and \texttt{anthropic/claude-haiku-5.5} and \texttt{openai/gpt-6-luna} via OpenRouter for Haiku and Luna, respectively. DeepSeek used temperature 0 and the other models used provider-default sampling, with reasoning disabled for all three. Seeds, software versions, endpoints and recorded request parameters are in the public repository (Data Availability Statement).

\subsection{Persistent exposure and directionality}\label{sec:exposure}
Exposure and current label correctness differ under persistence. All primary-window decisions in label and false-label had prior feedback exposure. Regime switches made labels stale in 756/2400 DeepSeek label prompts (31.50\%) and 372/1200 prompts for each replication (31.00\%). The same shares of false-label prompts carried a currently true label. For currently false persisted labels, equal-seed citation rates in label and false-label were 0.9907 and 0.9946 for DeepSeek, 0.7784 and 0.8314 for Haiku, and 0.9456 and 0.9568 for Luna (descriptive post-treatment strata).

Post hoc correctness strata produced different CSDs with the primary selectors retained: the current driver for label and its opposite for false-label. DeepSeek label CSD was $-0.2169$ [-0.2669, -0.1653] while the communicated label remained true and 0.3325 [0.2531, 0.4138] when stale. False-label CSD was $-0.3284$ [-0.3928, -0.2687] while its label remained false and 0.2983 [0.2295, 0.3630] when it became true. Among audited false-label decisions citing a currently false persisted label, fines occurred in 61/487 decisions for DeepSeek (12.53\%), 111/186 for Haiku (59.68\%) and 70/216 for Luna (32.41\%). Citing both drivers always passes the audit, so fines fall only on audited decisions that omit the current driver.

The post hoc directionality check reconstructed 23,970 valid primary feature re-asks. Among DeepSeek naive driver flips, 252/253 news actions and 466/466 momentum actions followed the reversed feature sign. Across all driver-probe decisions, the corresponding seed rates were 0.7019 and 0.8769. This check is mechanically related to baseline sign agreement (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

The public repository's exploratory outputs provide full decision flow, specificity, exposure strata, condition-by-regime rates and null-condition comparisons (Data Availability Statement).

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
\subsection{Interpreting separate behavioural outcomes}\label{sec:interpretation}
The results separate what agents cite from how their actions respond. For DeepSeek, false-label increased opposite-driver citation, but sensitivity increased more, yielding negative CSD without establishing improved explanations. Label increased true-driver citation without a conclusive precision gain, while evidence reduced true-driver sensitivity despite supplying return statistics. Citation alone therefore misses part of the observed response.

Failed news discrimination limits interpretation of these action changes as driver use. An oversight service therefore needs component rates alongside citation saturation, coverage and instrument diagnostics.

\subsection{Deploying a networked audit sidecar}\label{sec:network}
\noindent\textbf{Architecture.} A sidecar could receive the original prompt, validated action, reasons and context hash, then send single-feature sign reversals to the same requested model configuration. It would return component rates, coverage and null variability. Isolated queues would keep probes outside trade execution; timestamps would track feedback delivery and label correctness, and idempotent request identifiers would prevent duplicate probe processing during retries.

\noindent\textbf{Cost model.} A fully probed decision uses one original call, two sign-flip re-asks and, when selected, an identical-prompt re-ask. For probe probability $\rho$ and conditional null-selection probability $\nu$, expected calls per assigned decision are $1+\rho(2+\nu)$ before retries and invalid bases. Lower sampling reduces traffic but increases uncertainty and the risk of missing regime transitions.

\noindent\textbf{Measured workload.} The experiment's request log records every API attempt with token usage and charged cost where available. At the realized null share, full probing would require about 3.19 calls per decision for each model (Table~\ref{tab:workload}). Probing half of decisions required 2.10 call attempts per assigned decision for DeepSeek and 2.09 for Haiku and Luna. Request-log costs per 1,000 assigned decisions were USD 0.23, 0.29 and 0.17, respectively.

\noindent\textbf{Latency and non-determinism.} Latency was not measured: the request log has completion timestamps without per-request durations, and overlapping calls prevent interpreting timestamp gaps as service times. Sequential probes add their service times; parallel probes add the slowest time after the base, subject to queues and provider limits. An asynchronous sidecar would avoid probe delays in trading, although delayed feedback changes exposure. Identical-prompt action variability argues for continued monitoring after route changes.

\subsection{Mechanisms behind lower sign-flip sensitivity under the evidence package}\label{sec:mechanisms}
Evidence tables could contain associations from an earlier regime: five-observation windows mixed regimes in 294/718 DeepSeek primary audit tables (40.95\%) and 150/344 tables in each replication (43.60\%). Persistence carried these windows into 996/2400 DeepSeek primary prompts (41.50\%) and 549/1200 prompts in each replication (45.75\%). These associations could contribute to lower evidence sensitivity, including relative to punitive.

Requested-order impact also enters correlations through realized returns. Each agent contributes $0.001h_{it}q_{it}$, up to $\pm0.01$, before feasibility caps. Subtracting collective impact $0.03I_t$ from realized returns on the same observations gave a mean absolute correlation change of 0.2021 for DeepSeek, 0.1543 for Haiku and 0.1474 for Luna. This fixed-path calculation does not identify why feedback changed sensitivity.

\subsection{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}
Instrument validity limits interpretation of the measured action changes as driver use, especially where news-regime discrimination failed. The two-feature binary probe can miss interactions, thresholds and size changes, and create implausible contexts. With binary actions and sign reversal, agreement with the reversed sign conditional on a flip is mechanically equivalent to baseline sign agreement, so directionality provides no independent validation. Single re-asks cannot recover action probabilities; additive noise subtraction cannot separate perturbation effects from variability, identify individual effects, or adjust precision and recall. CSD lacks common-scale or behavioural ground-truth calibration and measures neither explanation completeness nor explanatory faithfulness; fidelity and audit agreement cannot independently validate those properties. Repeated re-asks and validation against known behaviour in each regime would be needed to establish what these measures detect.

Causal identification is limited by the non-factorial feedback packages and subsequent divergence in portfolios and histories. Punitive comparisons and fine associations cannot isolate label falsity, history length or numerical format. Because validity, nonempty sets and exposure strata arise after treatment, common-support and subgroup comparisons yield selected estimands. The five-observation evidence window leaves the registered eight-observation intervention unestimated. Fixed-path impact subtraction provides neither market resimulation nor mediation analysis and cannot establish a numerical-content mechanism or economic harm. Factorial package comparisons, prespecified handling of eligibility and exposure, and implementation of the registered evidence window would be needed to identify these effects.

External validity is limited by the synthetic market, forced trading, fixed agents and single prompt family. DeepSeek's both-driver citations (68--84\%) limit the diagnostic value of fidelity and near-ceiling recall because they do not distinguish the drivers. Model access routes and seed counts differ; no interaction was tested, so differing significance cannot establish heterogeneity attributable to model identity. The authors saw outcomes from development seeds before freezing the predictions and retained positive predictions; this prior exposure and the post hoc analyses further constrain generalization. Replications across markets, trading options, prompt families and controlled access routes, with audits that distinguish both-driver citation from specific attribution, would test how far the findings extend.

Reproducibility is limited because full raw response strings and immutable returned model versions were not archived, the effective sampling parameters were not logged, and the historical collection environment is incompletely recoverable. Failed Luna requests cannot establish the backend serving successful calls. Latency was not recorded, preventing assessment of optimized deployment performance. Future collection would need to archive responses, returned versions, effective settings, the execution environment and per-request durations to support exact reproduction and latency evaluation.

\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}
The results support the following conclusions within this design:
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
\item Audit feedback changed citations and action sensitivity by different amounts; measuring citations alone gives an incomplete picture of the observed response.
\item For DeepSeek, evidence lowered true-driver sign-flip sensitivity, and false-label increased opposite-driver sensitivity more than citation, yielding negative CSD; both results oppose the preregistered predictions.
\item In the replications, Luna showed lower precision and true-driver sensitivity under evidence; Haiku had no significant primary contrasts after Holm correction, and the DeepSeek false-label CSD result was not statistically confirmed in the replications' corrected tests.
\item The probe failed to discriminate the driver in the news regime for two of three models; per-regime instrument validation is required before interpreting these measures as driver use.
\item Probing half of decisions required about 2.10 call attempts per assigned decision, providing a measured workload for planning a networked audit service.
\end{enumerate}
